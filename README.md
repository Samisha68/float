# Float

**On-chain short-term credit for businesses whose money is delayed.**

> Float helps businesses turn tomorrow's revenue into today's opportunity.

---

## The problem

Small and medium businesses do not usually fail for lack of revenue. They fail because
the revenue arrives late.

An investor payment is in flight from another country. A customer invoice settles in
thirty days. A banking issue temporarily freezes access to an account. None of that moves
the deadlines that matter — salaries, vendors, rent — which arrive on schedule regardless.

That gap between money earned and money available is where otherwise-healthy businesses
run out of cash.

## What Float does

A verified business applies for short-term credit, supplying evidence of its incoming
funds, its financial activity, and its capacity to repay. Float underwrites that evidence
and, on approval, disburses **USDC** — targeting hours rather than the days a cross-border
bank transfer takes.

Every loan is recorded on **Solana**: disbursement, repayment schedule, interest, and
repayment status, all transparent and verifiable.

As a business repays, it accumulates a portable on-chain credit history. Over time that
record is what qualifies it for larger limits, better rates, and further products.

**For capital providers**, Float offers access to underwritten business credit, with yield
generated by real borrower repayments.

We start with short-term working-capital gaps. The longer arc is financial infrastructure
that helps small businesses manage cash flow, establish credibility, and grow.

## Direction

| | |
|---|---|
| **Customer** | Verified businesses with a documented, delayed inbound payment |
| **Product** | Short-term USDC working-capital credit |
| **Settlement** | Solana — disbursement, schedule, interest and status on-chain |
| **Capital** | Institutional / underwritten capital providers |
| **Compounding asset** | Borrower repayment history and the underwriting data behind it |
| **Later** | Larger facilities collateralised by RWAs and tokens |

### Open questions being worked

These are live design questions, not settled positions. They are written down so nobody
mistakes them for decided:

1. **Repayment control.** Evidence of an incoming payment is information, not control.
   Whether the inbound payment is routed through an account Float controls — versus trusting
   the borrower to remit on receipt — is the difference between factoring-grade and
   unsecured-grade loss rates.
2. **The moat.** Anything written to Solana is public by construction, so the repayment
   record itself is not proprietary. The defensible layer is off-chain: the evidence bundles,
   rejected applications, fraud labels, and loss curves.
3. **First capital.** Who funds the first book, on what terms, and behind whose first-loss.
4. **Jurisdiction.** Which market the first cohort of borrowers is underwritten in.

**The code in this repository predates the current direction.** It is a hackathon
prototype (MONOLITH Solana Mobile Hackathon) for a *consumer mobile* lending product that
is no longer what Float is building. It is kept for the on-chain patterns that carry over,
not as a statement of product intent.
