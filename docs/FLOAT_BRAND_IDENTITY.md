## Part 1 — Brand thesis

### What problem does Float solve?

Crypto has debit cards but no credit card. Every existing "crypto credit" product is
a pawnshop: lock $150 to borrow $100. The reason is that wallets are anonymous —
they have balances but no history, so every wallet is treated as a stranger forever.
Float builds the missing piece: a way for a wallet to **earn trust** — through
verified identity, provable income, and above all a repayment record — so that
collateral requirements fall over time, the way a real credit limit rises.

Underneath the consumer product is the company thesis:
**onchain wallets will need credit histories, not just asset balances.**
Float is the AI credit layer for onchain users.

### Who is the primary user?

Two sides, one phone:

- **Borrowers** — crypto-native individuals who hold assets (SOL, LSTs) but need
  short-term stablecoin liquidity without selling. They are mobile-first, global,
  often outside traditional credit systems. They are not degens seeking leverage;
  they need $50–$2,000 for real life.
- **Lenders** — everyday holders of digital dollars who want yield with clear,
  legible risk. They choose an agent-managed pool the way a saver chooses a
  savings product — not the way a trader picks a farm.

Both sides are trusting Float with money. That single fact should dominate every
design decision.

### How do DeFi, agentic AI, and Solana interact?

Three layers, strictly ordered:

1. **Solana + Anchor (the law).** Hard rules in code: loan caps, LTV limits,
   liquidation thresholds, insurance reserve. Non-negotiable, visible to all.
2. **Agents (the judgment).** AI curators that underwrite, match, monitor, remind,
   and liquidate — but only *inside* the law. The agent never invents risk; it
   exercises discretion within limits, like a loan officer bound by policy.
3. **DeFi mechanics (the market).** Pools, rates, collateral, repayment — the
   two-sided flow of capital that the first two layers make safe.

This hierarchy is the brand's most important idea: **autonomy inside guardrails.**
Not "AI decides your loan" (frightening) and not "code is law, good luck"
(cold). Intelligence, governed.

### What should users feel?

In order of priority:

1. **Safety** — "my money is held by something serious." The feeling of a
   well-run bank or a well-built ship, not a casino.
2. **Being trusted** — the emotional core for borrowers. Float is the first thing
   in crypto that says "we believe you'll pay it back." Progression on the
   ladder should feel like earned respect, not gamified points.
3. **Quiet competence** — agents work continuously in the background. The user
   should feel *attended to*, never *processed*.
4. **Buoyancy** — the name promises it. Lightness, staying above water, cash
   flow between paydays. The opposite of drowning in debt.

### What must the identity communicate?

**Trust first. Then intelligence. Then liquidity.** Coordination, autonomy, and
speed are supporting notes, expressed through motion and product behavior rather
than shouted in the logo. Explicitly *not* communicated: hype, gambling energy,
token-brain aesthetics, AI novelty. If the brand must choose between looking
innovative and looking dependable, it chooses dependable — innovation is proven
in the product, dependability must be signaled before a user deposits a cent.

**One-line brand thesis to design against:**

> Float is a calm, precise financial instrument that extends trust before
> collateral — intelligence working quietly inside hard rules, keeping you above
> the waterline.

## Part 2 — Ten logo concepts

A note on the name: "float" is already a banking term — the money in transit
between parties that the entire credit-card business is built on — and a
maritime one. The best marks below exploit that double meaning rather than
inventing a third.

---

### Concept 1 — Plimsoll

1. **Concept name:** Plimsoll
2. **Core symbol:** The Plimsoll line (International Load Line) — the circle
   bisected by a horizontal line painted on every cargo ship's hull.
3. **Background:** Introduced by Samuel Plimsoll's Merchant Shipping Act (1876)
   to stop "coffin ships" — overloaded vessels sent to sea to collect insurance.
   The mark shows at a glance the maximum safe load; if the waterline rises
   above it, the ship is overloaded. It is one of history's great trust marks:
   a public, standardized, verifiable statement of safe capacity that let
   insurers, sailors, and ports trust a vessel they'd never inspected.
4. **Connection to Float:** Float *is* a Plimsoll line for wallets — a public,
   rule-enforced statement of how much credit each wallet can safely carry.
   Loan caps, LTV limits, and the insurance reserve are literally load lines.
   And the mark means *float safely* — the product's name and promise in one glyph.
5. **DeFi:** The load line is transparent risk management made visible —
   exactly what on-chain collateral rules and liquidation thresholds are.
6. **Agentic AI:** The flanking gradations of a real load line (TF, F, T, S, W)
   mark different conditions — the agent's job is reading conditions and setting
   the safe line. The mark is the *output* of continuous intelligent assessment.
7. **Solana, subtly:** The gradation ticks beside the circle can be drawn as
   three short horizontal bars — a quiet echo of Solana's three-bar mark without
   borrowing its gradient or angle.
8. **Construction:** A perfect circle, a horizontal line through its center
   extending slightly beyond both edges, and two or three short tick marks
   stacked to the right. Single stroke weight, geometric, drawable with a
   compass and straightedge.
9. **As app icon:** Deep navy tile, the circle-and-line in warm off-white with
   the waterline tick in signal orange. Reads clearly at 16 px.
10. **Risks:** The circle-and-crossbar could be mistaken for a "no entry" sign or
    a Ø if proportions are careless (the line must not extend far past the
    circle and must be horizontal). The maritime story needs one sentence of
    telling before it clicks.
11. **Image prompt:** "Minimal geometric logo: a perfect circle bisected by a
    single horizontal line extending slightly past its edges, three short
    horizontal tick marks stacked to the right of the circle, single uniform
    stroke weight, off-white mark on deep navy background, one tick highlighted
    in burnt orange, flat vector, no gradients, maritime load-line reference,
    Swiss design precision."

### Concept 2 — Statera

1. **Concept name:** Statera
2. **Core symbol:** The Roman steelyard balance (*statera*) — the unequal-arm
   scale where a small sliding counterweight balances a much heavier load.
3. **Background:** The statera was the working instrument of Roman commerce,
   found in every market from Pompeii to Londinium. Unlike the symmetric scales
   of justice, it balances a large cargo against a *small* weight through
   leverage — position on the calibrated arm does the work.
4. **Connection to Float:** The exact mechanics of Float's ladder: a small
   amount of proven trust, correctly positioned, balances a large loan.
   Reputation is leverage. As the borrower's record grows, the counterweight
   slides further — less collateral, more credit.
5. **DeFi:** A calibrated, public instrument of value exchange — measurement
   anyone can verify, the ancestor of the transparent rate curve.
6. **Agentic AI:** Someone must read the arm and place the weight. The agent is
   the unseen hand positioning the counterweight — continuous, precise
   calibration.
7. **Solana, subtly:** The calibration notches on the arm can be three evenly
   spaced marks; the arm itself can sit at Solana's characteristic slight
   oblique.
8. **Construction:** A horizontal beam off-center on a single pivot point; a
   large circle hanging close to the pivot, a small filled dot far along the
   arm. Three elements: line, circle, dot.
9. **As app icon:** The beam-circle-dot mark centered on a dark tile; the small
   counterweight dot in accent orange — the eye goes to the small thing doing
   the big work.
10. **Risks:** Related to the "scales" cliché of legal/finance brands — the
    asymmetry must be exaggerated to read as steelyard, not scales of justice.
    Balance imagery is common; execution has to carry it.
11. **Image prompt:** "Minimal logo of a Roman steelyard balance: one horizontal
    beam on an off-center pivot, large outlined circle hanging near the pivot,
    small solid dot at the far end of the arm, three tick marks on the beam,
    flat single-weight vector, off-white on deep navy, small dot in burnt
    orange, geometric, no gradients, ancient-instrument-meets-Swiss-modernism."

### Concept 3 — Tessera

1. **Concept name:** Tessera
2. **Core symbol:** The *tessera hospitalis* / Greek *symbolon* — a token broken
   in two, each party keeping half; rejoining the halves proved the bond.
3. **Background:** In the ancient Mediterranean, contracts of hospitality and
   credit were sealed by snapping a bone or clay tablet in two. Years later, a
   stranger presenting the matching half was owed the obligation. It is the
   etymological root of "symbol" — and the conceptual root of every credential
   and key pair since.
4. **Connection to Float:** Every loan is two halves matched: borrower and
   lender, brought together by the agent. The break line is unique — like a
   cryptographic pair, only the true counterpart fits.
5. **DeFi:** Trustless verification embodied: the proof *is* the object. No
   authority needed to validate the match — exactly the promise of on-chain
   attestation.
6. **Agentic AI:** The agent is the matchmaker that finds and joins the halves —
   agent_match_loan as a physical gesture.
7. **Solana, subtly:** The break line can be drawn as a stepped fracture of
   three segments — three-bar echo inside the seam itself.
8. **Construction:** A rounded square or circle split by an irregular stepped
   seam into two interlocking halves, slightly offset so the gap reads. Two
   filled shapes, one negative-space line.
9. **As app icon:** The two halves nearly joined on a dark tile, the seam
   glowing in accent color — a mark that literally depicts the moment of a
   match.
10. **Risks:** Split-shape marks are common in fintech (payments = two parties);
    the fracture must look deliberately *keyed*, not just a broken circle.
    Halves that read as "broken" could subconsciously suggest something failed.
11. **Image prompt:** "Minimal logo: a rounded square broken into two
    interlocking halves by a stepped zigzag seam of three segments, halves
    slightly pulled apart, flat solid shapes in off-white on deep navy, seam
    gap revealing burnt orange, flat vector, no gradients, ancient broken-token
    tessera hospitalis reference, precise geometric construction."

### Concept 4 — Tally

1. **Concept name:** Tally
2. **Core symbol:** The medieval split tally stick — a wooden stick notched
   with the amount of a debt, then split lengthwise into the *stock* (lender's
   half) and *foil* (borrower's half).
3. **Background:** For six centuries the English Exchequer ran state credit on
   tally sticks. The notches recorded the amount; the split made forgery
   impossible because the grain and notches had to match. The lender "held the
   stock" — the origin of *stockholder*. Tallies circulated as money: debt
   records as liquid instruments.
4. **Connection to Float:** The tally is the ancestor of Float's core asset — a
   tamper-proof repayment record that both sides hold. Every repaid loan is a
   new notch; the accumulated notches *are* the credit history.
5. **DeFi:** An unforgeable distributed ledger, five hundred years early. Two
   parties, matching records, no central book to corrupt.
6. **Agentic AI:** The agent is the Exchequer clerk — cutting the notches,
   reading histories at a glance, extending terms based on the record.
7. **Solana, subtly:** Three notches as the canonical mark count.
8. **Construction:** A vertical rounded bar with three notches cut into one
   edge, split by a hairline into two slightly offset halves. Reads
   simultaneously as a tally, a bar chart, and an "F" spine.
9. **As app icon:** The notched bar centered on a dark tile — strong vertical
   presence, notches in accent color, instantly ownable.
10. **Risks:** Could read as a generic bar/equalizer glyph if the split isn't
    visible at small size; the historical story is wonderful but obscure —
    needs telling.
11. **Image prompt:** "Minimal logo: a vertical rounded rectangle with three
    square notches cut into its right edge, split lengthwise by a thin offset
    seam into two halves, flat off-white on deep navy, notches in burnt
    orange, flat vector, no gradients, medieval split tally stick reference,
    modernist reduction."

### Concept 5 — Displacement

1. **Concept name:** Displacement
2. **Core symbol:** Archimedes' principle — a body floats by displacing its own
   weight; rendered as a circle riding a waterline.
3. **Background:** Archimedes discovered buoyancy while solving a *trust*
   problem: was the king's crown honest gold? Displacement was history's first
   cryptographic proof of value — verify the substance without destroying it.
   A body floats exactly as high as its integrity allows.
4. **Connection to Float:** You float on what you've proven. The more weight of
   trust a wallet carries, the higher it rides above the waterline — the ladder
   as physics. And "verify the crown without melting it" is precisely zkTLS:
   prove income without revealing documents.
5. **DeFi:** Liquidity as an actual liquid — the pool, the waterline,
   equilibrium. Collateral is ballast; reputation is buoyancy.
6. **Agentic AI:** The system continuously finds equilibrium without a central
   commander — the way water levels itself. The agent maintains everyone's safe
   waterline.
7. **Solana, subtly:** The submerged arc can be sliced into three horizontal
   bands — three bars, underwater.
8. **Construction:** A circle intersected by a horizontal line: the arc above
   the line is a solid stroke, the arc below is broken into dashes or bands
   (the submerged portion). One circle, one line, two treatments.
9. **As app icon:** Circle-on-waterline centered on deep navy; above-water arc
   in off-white, submerged arc in muted blue. Serene and immediate.
10. **Risks:** Closest neighbor to sunrise/horizon clichés and to Concept 1's
    geometry — if both survive, only one may. Must avoid reading as an eye or a
    setting sun.
11. **Image prompt:** "Minimal logo: a circle intersected by a horizontal
    waterline, upper arc drawn as a solid clean stroke, lower arc rendered as
    three horizontal dashed bands suggesting submersion, flat vector, off-white
    and muted steel blue on deep navy, no gradients, Archimedes buoyancy
    reference, calm geometric precision."

### Concept 6 — Murmuration

1. **Concept name:** Murmuration
2. **Core symbol:** A starling murmuration — thousands of birds producing
   coherent, fluid motion from three simple local rules.
3. **Background:** Murmurations are the canonical natural example of emergent
   coordination: no leader, no central plan, yet the flock moves as one and
   evades predators no individual could see. Boids (1986) showed three rules —
   separation, alignment, cohesion — generate the whole behavior. It is the
   founding metaphor of swarm intelligence.
4. **Connection to Float:** Agent-managed pools are a murmuration: autonomous
   agents each following hard local rules, producing system-level safety and
   liquidity no single actor coordinates.
5. **DeFi:** Decentralization drawn honestly — order *without* a center, from
   rules everyone can read.
6. **Agentic AI:** The most direct representation on this list: autonomous
   agents, simple constraints, emergent intelligence.
7. **Solana, subtly:** Compose the flock from exactly the tapered-bar geometry
   of Solana's mark — but scattered into flight, at most three or five marks.
8. **Construction:** Five (or three) short tapered strokes of varying size
   arranged along an implied rising S-curve, whitespace doing the work of
   motion. Not a bird illustration — pure abstract strokes in formation.
9. **As app icon:** Three-to-five strokes sweeping up-right across a dark tile;
   at 16 px reads as a dynamic checkmark-like gesture.
10. **Risks:** Flocks/particles are the most-used visual in AI branding —
    execution must be unusually disciplined to avoid the "generic swarm
    startup" look. Also inherently less *solid* — motion connotes flightiness,
    which fights the trust mandate. Weakest at conveying custody of money.
11. **Image prompt:** "Minimal abstract logo: five short tapered strokes of
    varying length arranged in a rising S-curve flight formation like a
    starling murmuration, flat off-white strokes on deep navy, single stroke
    in burnt orange leading the formation, flat vector, no gradients, generous
    negative space, disciplined Swiss composition, not literal birds."

### Concept 7 — Bend

1. **Concept name:** Bend
2. **Core symbol:** The sheet bend — the sailor's knot specifically designed to
   join two ropes of *different* thicknesses securely.
3. **Background:** Knots are humanity's oldest trust technology — the word
   "bond" (financial and personal) descends from *binding*. The sheet bend is
   notable because it is the correct knot for *unequal* partners: a thick rope
   and a thin one, joined so that load makes the joint tighter, not looser.
4. **Connection to Float:** A lender with capital and a borrower with little —
   unequal parties joined by structure so that stress strengthens the bond.
   Repayment pressure tightens trust rather than breaking it. Also natively
   maritime, keeping the Float name-world coherent.
5. **DeFi:** The bond/knot is a contract made physical — visible structure
   holding value, undone only by the correct deliberate action.
6. **Agentic AI:** The agent ties the knot — chooses the right joining
   structure for each pair of unequal parties.
7. **Solana, subtly:** The interlaced path can be drawn with three visible
   strand segments at the crossing.
8. **Construction:** Two rounded open loops of different stroke weights
   interlocked in the sheet-bend topology, heavily simplified to two
   interlocking U-shapes — almost a monogram. Continuous line logic, flat.
9. **As app icon:** The two-weight interlock centered on dark; the thin strand
   in accent color. Distinctive silhouette at any size.
10. **Risks:** Interlocking-loop marks live near Mastercard/chain-link
    territory; must keep the *unequal stroke weights* — that asymmetry is the
    whole idea and the differentiator. Complex topology can muddy below 24 px.
11. **Image prompt:** "Minimal logo of a simplified sheet bend knot: two
    interlocking U-shaped rope loops, one thick stroke and one thin stroke,
    flat vector, clean over-under crossings, off-white thick strand and burnt
    orange thin strand on deep navy, no gradients, nautical knot reduced to
    geometric monogram, precise and calm."

### Concept 8 — Hallmark

1. **Concept name:** Hallmark
2. **Core symbol:** The assay hallmark — the row of small punched marks stamped
   into precious metal certifying maker, purity, and assay office.
3. **Background:** Hallmarking (Goldsmiths' Hall, London, 1300s — arguably the
   world's oldest consumer-protection system) let strangers trade gold and
   silver without personally testing it. A tiny row of stamps carried the full
   chain of attestation: who made it, who verified it, what it's worth.
4. **Connection to Float:** Float's ladder *is* a hallmark row: personhood
   stamp, income stamp, repayment stamps — attestations accumulating on a
   wallet, each one earned, together making it trustable by strangers.
   Solana Attestation Service is literally a digital assay office.
5. **DeFi:** Verification by mark rather than by authority-in-the-room; the
   stamp travels with the asset the way attestations travel with the wallet.
6. **Agentic AI:** The assayer is the agent — the examiner that tests and
   stamps. Each mark is a decision recorded permanently.
7. **Solana, subtly:** Three stamps in the row. The attestation reference *is*
   the Solana-ecosystem tie.
8. **Construction:** A horizontal row of three small distinct punched shapes —
   e.g., a circle, a shield-square, an "F" lozenge — enclosed in soft-cornered
   frames, like a real hallmark strip. The lozenge-F can also stand alone as
   the compact mark.
9. **As app icon:** The single F-in-lozenge punch mark, debossed-feeling, on a
   dark tile; the full three-stamp row lives on the website and documents.
10. **Risks:** A row of marks is a *system*, not a single memorable glyph — the
    standalone lozenge must carry the icon duty. Multiple shapes risk fussiness
    at small sizes. Debossed/stamped rendering tempts skeuomorphism.
11. **Image prompt:** "Minimal logo system: a horizontal row of three small
    punched hallmark stamps — a circle mark, a shield mark, and a letter F in
    an elongated lozenge — each inside a soft-cornered rectangular frame, flat
    off-white on deep navy, the F lozenge in burnt orange, flat vector, no
    gradients, inspired by precious-metal assay hallmarks, engraved-punch
    character but strictly flat and geometric."

### Concept 9 — Governor

1. **Concept name:** Governor
2. **Core symbol:** Watt's centrifugal governor — the paired spinning weights
   that automatically throttle a steam engine, the first famous autonomous
   feedback controller.
3. **Background:** The flyball governor (1788) is the founding artifact of
   control theory and the ancestor of every autonomous system: a machine that
   observes itself and corrects itself, *within limits set by its builder*.
   "Cybernetics" comes from *kybernētēs* — helmsman — the same root as
   "governor." Engineering's original symbol of trustworthy autonomy.
4. **Connection to Float:** Float's exact architecture: agents regulate lending
   speed, exposure, and limits automatically — but the geometry of the machine
   (the hard on-chain rules) bounds what they can ever do. Autonomy inside
   guardrails, as a mechanism.
5. **DeFi:** Rate curves and utilization mechanics *are* governors —
   self-adjusting economic throttles. The mark depicts the protocol's honest
   self-regulation.
6. **Agentic AI:** The strongest "trustworthy autonomy" symbol available: not a
   brain, not a bot — a *regulator*. Intelligence expressed as balance in
   motion.
7. **Solana, subtly:** The central spindle plus two arms gives three strokes;
   the arms' splay can sit at the Solana oblique.
8. **Construction:** A vertical center line with two arms splayed symmetrically
   downward-outward, each ending in a solid ball — like a "Y" inverted, or a
   plumb figure. Three lines, two dots. Pleasingly close to an abstract
   ascending figure with arms out — reads faintly human without being a mascot.
9. **As app icon:** The splayed-arm figure centered on dark; balls in accent
   color. Symmetric, stable, unusual.
10. **Risks:** The reference is obscure — most users will see an abstract
    figure/anchor/Y and need the story. Symmetric splayed forms can drift
    toward "generic person logo" (human-resources energy) if the balls are
    lost.
11. **Image prompt:** "Minimal logo of a Watt centrifugal governor: a vertical
    spindle line with two symmetric arms angled downward and outward, each arm
    ending in a solid circular ball weight, flat single-weight vector,
    off-white lines with burnt orange ball weights on deep navy, no gradients,
    18th-century flyball governor abstracted to five geometric elements, calm
    and symmetric."

### Concept 10 — Lock

1. **Concept name:** Lock
2. **Core symbol:** The canal lock — the chambered gate system that lifts
   vessels stepwise between water levels.
3. **Background:** Canal locks (perfected by Leonardo's miter gate, 1497) made
   inland trade possible: a boat enters a chamber, the chamber fills, the boat
   *rises*, the gate opens to the higher level. Trade networks of the 17th–19th
   centuries ran on staircases of locks lifting cargo over entire watersheds —
   patient, controlled, stepwise elevation by liquidity itself.
4. **Connection to Float:** The credit ladder as hydraulics: each repaid loan
   fills the chamber; the borrower rises to the next level — bigger limits,
   less collateral. Elevation is *earned in steps*, never jumped. Liquidity is
   literally what lifts you.
5. **DeFi:** Pooled liquidity doing productive work under strict gating rules —
   funds flow between levels only when conditions are met, like escrow made
   architectural.
6. **Agentic AI:** The lock-keeper — the agent that watches levels, opens gates
   in the right order, and never opens both ends at once (risk isolation as
   physical protocol).
7. **Solana, subtly:** Three chamber levels drawn as three ascending horizontal
   bars — a natural, honest three-bar echo.
8. **Construction:** Three horizontal bars ascending stepwise left-to-right
   (rising water levels), enclosed by two subtle vertical gate strokes; or
   reduced further to just the three ascending water-bars with a small circle
   (the vessel) riding the top one.
9. **As app icon:** Three ascending bars with the small floating circle on the
   highest — reads as "rising levels + something floating," legible at 16 px.
10. **Risks:** Ascending bars flirt with the forbidden bar-chart cliché — the
    enclosing gates or the floating vessel-dot are what keep it a *lock* and
    not a chart. The word "lock" is an unfortunate collision with "locked
    funds" in crypto vocabulary (naming: call the concept "Levels" if adopted).
11. **Image prompt:** "Minimal logo: three horizontal water-level bars
    ascending in steps from left to right, a small circle floating on the
    highest bar, flanked by two thin vertical gate lines, flat vector,
    off-white bars and burnt orange floating circle on deep navy, no
    gradients, canal lock chambers abstracted, calm stepwise rhythm."

### The strongest three

**1. Plimsoll (82).** The rare case where name, story, and product mechanics
converge on one mark. It is *about floating*, it is *about safe credit
capacity*, it is a genuine historical trust mark with the exact social function
Float performs, and it reduces to compass-and-straightedge geometry that will
survive twenty years and 16 pixels. Its only real weakness — weak direct AI
symbolism — is acceptable because agentic intelligence is better expressed
through product behavior and motion than through a static glyph.

**2. Statera (73).** The best *mechanical* metaphor for the ladder: small
proven trust balancing large credit through position, not mass. Distinctive
silhouette, honest classical pedigree, quietly implies the agent as the hand on
the counterweight. Slightly hurt by adjacency to scales-of-justice clichés.

**3. Tally (72) / Hallmark (71) — effectively tied.** Tally is the most
*historically precise* reference on the list (a split, unforgeable, circulating
credit record is Float's entire long-term asset) and yields the most ownable
vertical mark. Hallmark is strategically brilliant for the attestation ladder
and dovetails with Solana Attestation Service, but is a system of marks rather
than one icon, which costs it the tiebreak.

Murmuration scores highest on agentic representation and lowest overall — a
useful proof that maximizing the AI signal minimizes the trust signal. The
brand should not lead with the agent.

### Final recommendation — Plimsoll

Adopt **Plimsoll** as the primary mark, and borrow one idea from Hallmark for
the system around it.

**Refinement into a logo system:**

- **The mark.** Circle Ø-height equal to the wordmark cap-height ×1.4; waterline
  precisely horizontal, extending past the circle by ¼ radius each side; three
  gradation ticks right of the circle at fixed intervals. Single stroke weight
  matched to the wordmark's stem weight. Test at 16/24/48/512 px; below 24 px,
  drop the ticks automatically (a defined "compact mark").
- **The wordmark.** "Float" set lowercase in the brand grotesk, slightly
  extended tracking; the crossbar of the lowercase *t* aligned to the mark's
  waterline — one continuous horizon through the lockup.
- **The living waterline.** The horizontal line is the brand's kinetic element:
  in-product, each user's mark can show *their* waterline — rising with
  repayment history. The logo literally becomes the credit ladder. This is
  where agentic intelligence enters the identity: the line is set by the agent,
  and it moves.
- **The tick system (from Hallmark).** The three gradation ticks double as the
  attestation indicators — personhood, income, repayment — filling in accent
  color as the user earns them. Marketing uses the empty marks; a trusted
  user's app shows theirs filled.
- **Clear space** equal to the circle's radius on all sides; never rotate the
  waterline; never gradient the mark; monochrome versions in ink and in paper.

## Part 4 — Color system

### Strategy

The palette comes from the world the mark comes from: **deep water, chart
paper, and signal orange** — the visual language of marine navigation and
insurance, which is to say: centuries of communicating *safety around money and
cargo*. It is warm where fintech is clinical, dark where banks are beige, and
completely free of the purple–green gradient axis that codes "Solana clone" —
while the three-tick motif keeps the ecosystem kinship. No competitor in the
Solana lending set (Kamino teal, Jupiter green-cyan, marginfi yellow-green,
Phantom purple) owns navy + signal orange.

Psychology: deep desaturated navy is the most durable trust color in finance,
but pushing it *dark* (toward ink, not corporate royal blue) reads modern and
technical rather than bureaucratic. The warm paper white keeps light mode from
feeling like a hospital. Orange is used the way the sea uses it — scarce,
high-visibility, meaning *"important, look here"* — which keeps it trustworthy
instead of playful.

| Role | Name | HEX | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary brand | Deepwater | `#0B2740` | Brand mass: logo tiles, light-mode primary buttons, headers, footers, marketing backgrounds |
| Secondary brand | Harbor | `#3E7CB9` | Links, selected states, informational accents, chart series 1 |
| Accent | Plimsoll Orange | `#E8632C` | The waterline tick, primary CTA in dark mode, progress on the ladder, "your line" in charts. Ration it: ≤5% of any screen |
| Background (dark, default) | Fathom | `#0A1622` | App background, dark mode |
| Surface (dark) | — | `#12202F` | Cards, sheets |
| Surface raised (dark) | — | `#1A2B3D` | Modals, popovers, active cards |

### Accessibility

- Body text pairs are AA-clean: `#F2EFE9` on `#0A1622` (~15:1), `#14202E` on
  `#F7F5F0` (~13:1). Secondary text colors are tuned to stay ≥4.5:1 on their
  backgrounds.
- **Plimsoll Orange `#E8632C` is a graphic color, not a text color** on dark
  (~4.1:1) — use it for strokes, fills, ticks, and large numerals only; for
  orange *text* on Fathom use tint `#F0824F`. On light backgrounds use the
  darker `#C64F1E` for text.
- Semantic colors always ship with an icon or label — never color-only status
  (colorblind safety; green/red pairs especially).
- Every color has one job. If orange starts appearing in three places on one
  screen, the screen is wrong.

## Part 5 — Product theme

### The big calls

| Decision | Call | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mode | **Dark-first, adaptive.** Dark is default (crypto-native audience, OLED phones, money-at-night reality); a true light theme ships day one for docs, web, and daylight | A serious financial OS must not be dark-only — that's the casino tell |
| Visual language | **Institutional-technical** — the confidence of a bank statement with the precision of an instrument panel. Editorial only on the landing page | Users are trusting Float with money before they trust it with attention |
| Corners | **Rounded, restrained: 10px cards, 8px buttons, 6px inputs.** Never pill-shaped cards, never sharp 0px | Full-sharp reads brutalist/experimental; full-round reads toy |
| Density | **Spacious by default, dense on demand.** Consumer screens breathe; lender analytics may tighten | Borrowers are consumers; lenders are semi-pro |
| Motion | **Motion-led, but liquid and slow.** Physics of water: things settle, level, and rise — nothing bounces, nothing spins | Motion is where "agentic" lives; it must feel like competence, not excitement |
| Imagery | **Diagrams and data, zero illustration mascots.** Real product UI in marketing | Illustration systems age fast and read startup-generic |
| Web3 visibility | **Subtle.** Addresses truncated behind names, gas abstracted, "on-chain" as a proof detail (view on explorer) not a headline | The point is credit, not chain |

### Typography

- **UI + product: a precise grotesk with tabular figures** — Suisse Int'l
  (budget alternative: Inter, tightened). Weights 400/500/600 only.
- **Numerals are the brand's second voice:** all money and rates in tabular
  lining figures, generously sized. A balance should be the most beautiful
  thing on the screen.
- **Mono for the chain layer:** IBM Plex Mono for addresses, hashes, tx IDs —
  a deliberate register-switch that says "you are now looking at the machine."
- **Landing page display: an editorial serif** (e.g., Tiempos Headline) for
  headlines only — the voice of an institution, over product-UI screenshots.
- Type scale 1.250 ratio: 12 / 14 / 16 / 20 / 25 / 31 / 39 / 49.

### Grid, spacing, components

- **4px base unit; 8/12/16/24/32/48 spacing steps.** Mobile margins 20px;
  web content max-width 1120px on a 12-column grid.
- **Buttons:** primary = filled Deepwater (light) / Plimsoll Orange (dark,
  reserved for the one action that matters per screen); secondary = 1px
  outline; destructive = filled Breach with mandatory confirm. Height 48px
  mobile. No gradients on interactive elements, ever.
- **Cards:** flat fill + 1px border, no drop shadows in dark mode (elevation =
  lighter surface); light mode may use one soft shadow level. Radius 10px.
  Left-edge 2px status rail (Solvent/Ballast/Breach) on loan cards.
- **Navigation:** mobile = 4-tab bottom bar (Borrow · Lend · Activity ·
  Profile), center-weighted, no hamburger; web = slim top bar, docs-style left
  rail inside dashboards.
- **The waterline as UI element:** a thin horizontal rule that appears at key
  moments — under the balance header, as the loan-health indicator, as the
  ladder progress line. One motif, systemically reused; this is the brand in
  the product.

### Dashboard & data visualization

- Lender dashboard leads with three numbers before any chart: **deposited,
  earned, pool health.** Charts explain; numbers answer.
- Line and area charts with flat fills (Harbor series, orange only for "you" /
  thresholds); no glow, no gradient fills, no candlesticks anywhere.
- Every APY shown with its basis ("last 30d, net of fees") — precision as a
  trust cue.
- Ladder visualization: horizontal waterline with the user's mark riding it;
  next level requirements stated in plain language ("2 more on-time
  repayments").

### Iconography

- 1.5px stroke geometric set (Lucide as base), 24px grid, no filled icons
  except active tab states. Custom icons only for domain objects: the mark, the
  ticks, pool, agent, attestation stamps.
- The agent's icon is the **governor/tiller motif — never a robot, sparkle, or
  brain.** Sparkle-AI iconography is banned; it now codes "generic AI feature."

### Motion principles

1. **Water physics:** ease-out-quint settles, 250–350ms; loading = a level
   line filling, not spinners.
2. **The rise is sacred:** ladder promotions get the one deliberate 600ms
   moment — the waterline rises to the new level and holds. The only
   celebratory animation in the app; no confetti anywhere.
3. **Agent presence = calm pulse:** when the agent is evaluating, a slow
   1.5s breathing opacity on the status line — visible work, zero drama —
   always resolving to a written outcome ("Approved · $75 · 7 days · why?").
4. **Money movements confirm with weight:** a short settle-down motion on
   balance changes; errors never shake.

### Empty states, trust cues, security

- Empty states teach mechanics in two sentences, then one action. First-run
  borrower empty state shows the ladder itself — the product's promise *is*
  the onboarding.
- **Trust surface:** a permanent, boring "Protocol" page — reserve size, hard
  caps, audit links, program address, agent constraint list ("what agents can
  never do"). Written like a fact sheet, not marketing.
- Every agent decision has a human-readable "why" trail. Every balance links
  quietly to the explorer. Fees shown before signing, always in absolute
  dollars first.
- Security patterns: system-standard biometric sheets, explicit signing
  summaries in plain language ("You are locking 0.8 SOL to borrow 60 USDC"),
  no dark patterns on repayment.

### Landing page art direction

- Chart Paper background, editorial serif headline, one line: **"Your wallet
  deserves a credit line."** Sub: borrowers earn trust, lenders earn yield.
- Hero visual: the real app, dark UI on device, showing a waterline rising —
  no 3D coins, no floating cubes, no glassmorphism, no gradient meshes.
- Below the fold: the ladder as an honest diagram; the three attestation
  ticks; the fact-sheet trust section; live protocol numbers (loans issued,
  repayment rate) once real.
- Footer in Deepwater with the full hallmark row of the identity: mark +
  wordmark + program address in Plex Mono.
